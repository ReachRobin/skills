# Example Roasts

No real names. No company names. Just the title and context.

---

## "Growth Hacker" | Early-stage startup, 15 employees

**Honest translation:** A title that peaked in 2014 and never fully recovered. The actual job is running paid acquisition experiments, setting up drip sequences, and writing copy. "Hacker" was supposed to signal scrappiness. It now signals that the job posting was written in 2017 and no one updated it.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Manages the Google Ads account and the Meta Ads account and the LinkedIn Ads account, all at the same time
- A/B tests subject lines
- Builds the Zapier automations that everyone depends on and nobody documents
- Writes the monthly "state of growth" slide for the all-hands

**Trajectory:** Came from a digital marketing agency or a slightly larger startup where they did the same job with a budget. Going next to "Director of Growth" at a Series A company, or leaving to start their own growth consultancy and posting about it on LinkedIn.

## "Revenue Operations Manager" | B2B SaaS, 200 employees

**Honest translation:** The person who actually knows how the CRM is configured and why. Nominally a strategy role. In practice, the person the sales team yells at when a report is wrong, and the person the CEO yells at when the board deck numbers don't match Salesforce. Neither of these is their fault.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Maintains the Salesforce instance that nobody wants to admit is 60% technical debt
- Builds the pipeline coverage dashboards everyone looks at and nobody acts on
- Writes and enforces the data hygiene rules that reps ignore
- Sits in on every forecast call without being asked for an opinion

**Trajectory:** Came from sales ops or a BizOps analyst role. Going to "Director of Revenue Operations" at a larger company, or "VP RevOps" at a company that's about to hire a real VP and needs someone to hold the chair first.

## "Head of Customer Success" | PLG SaaS, 80 employees, series A

**Honest translation:** Customer support with a quota and a fancier Slack channel. The "Head of" means they manage 2-3 CSMs and report to whoever the CRO is this year. The "success" framing means they own net revenue retention in theory and explain why churn happened in practice.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Runs the QBR template and sends it to accounts who don't show up
- Manages escalations that the support team couldn't close
- Builds the health score model that's never quite finished
- Tries to get product to fix the thing that three churned customers mentioned

**Trajectory:** Came from account management or enterprise support. Going to VP of CS at a later-stage company, or to "Chief Customer Officer" at a startup that's technically too small to have a Chief anything.

## "Principal Engineer" | Enterprise software, 2,000 employees

**Honest translation:** A Staff Engineer who either didn't want to manage people or tried it once and came back. Does the work that architects specify but aren't implementing, and implements the things architects haven't specified yet. The "Principal" means they're senior enough to push back on product in architecture review and win occasionally.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Writes the design docs that engineers actually follow
- Reviews PRs for the three teams that touch the same infrastructure
- Gets pulled into every "critical path" project the week before its deadline
- Explains to the VP of Engineering why the migration is going to take longer than the VP told the board

**Trajectory:** Came up through senior/staff IC track; skipped or left engineering management. Going to Distinguished Engineer at a larger company, CTO at an early-stage startup, or staying exactly here for 7 more years because the equity refresh keeps landing.

## "Evangelist" | Developer tools startup, 60 employees

**Honest translation:** Marketing, but for an audience that doesn't respond to marketing. The actual job is talking at conferences, writing technical blog posts, maintaining a personal brand that's technically the company's brand, and answering questions on Stack Overflow that are actually product gaps. Paid to have opinions in public.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Speaks at 8-12 conferences per year and writes a recap thread for each
- Builds demo apps that live in a GitHub repo nobody forks
- Does YouTube tutorials that the team calls "content strategy"
- Translates product announcements from "we shipped a feature" into "this changes everything" without technically lying

**Trajectory:** Came from an engineering or DevRel background with a Twitter following. Going to VP of DevRel at a larger company, or "founder" with a Substack and a consulting practice.

## "Chief of Staff" | High-growth startup, 120 employees, Series B

**Honest translation:** The CEO's second brain and first draft. The scope is everything the CEO doesn't have time for and nothing that belongs to anyone else yet. In month 1 it's "strategic partner." By month 6 it's project manager, internal comms writer, board prep coordinator, and the person who schedules the thing everyone keeps missing.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Owns the weekly company meeting agenda and the materials no one submits on time
- Runs the OKR process nobody believes in but everyone does
- Prepares the board deck slides, then revises them once the CEO looks at them
- Manages the 14 cross-functional projects that don't have a home on any org chart

**Trajectory:** Often a first job out of a top MBA, or a high-performer from within who the CEO pulled in. Goes to a COO role at a smaller company, a VP of Strategy role at a larger one, or CEO of their own startup once they've seen enough of how one is run.

## "Chief Vibes Officer" | Any company that announced this

**Honest translation:** Not a job. A LinkedIn post. The company needed content; someone's title became the content. The actual work - if any work exists - is team events, internal culture programming, and whatever "culture" means at this company this quarter. The title will not appear on the next company org chart.

**Real responsibilities:**
- Plans the offsite that has a yoga session and a strategy session and nobody's sure which one is the point
- Answers "what's the vibe right now" in all-hands Q&A
- Has strong opinions about the Slack emoji reaction set

**Trajectory:** Returns to a real title at a real company. The LinkedIn post got 40,000 impressions. The role lasted 14 months.

# Example: Auditing a bad 5-touch SDR cadence

## Inputs

**Cadence:**

---

Touch 1 | LinkedIn invite note | Day 1

> Hi {{first_name}}, hope this message finds you well! I came across your profile and was really impressed with your work as a thought leader in the SaaS space. We help companies like yours accelerate growth and scale faster. I'd love to connect and share how we've helped similar businesses achieve amazing results. Looking forward to connecting!

---

Touch 2 | LinkedIn DM | Day 3

> Hi {{first_name}}! Just wanted to bump this up in case it got buried. Would love to chat about how we can help [Company] achieve its goals. Let me know if you have 30 minutes this week!

---

Touch 3 | LinkedIn DM | Day 5

> Hey {{first_name}}, reaching back out because I truly believe our solution could be a game-changer for your team. We work with companies just like yours to drive efficiency and growth at scale. Our platform has helped hundreds of businesses transform their operations. Would you be open to a quick 30-minute call to explore if there's a fit?

---

Touch 4 | Email | Day 7

> Subject: Quick question for you

> Hi {{first_name}}, I'll keep this brief. I wanted to reach out one more time because I know how busy you are and I wouldn't want you to miss out on something that could really make a difference for your business. Our platform helps companies in the SaaS space streamline their processes, reduce costs, and unlock growth they didn't think was possible. We've worked with companies like Acme Corp and XYZ Inc to deliver real, measurable results. I know you're probably getting dozens of messages like this, but I genuinely think this is different. Could we get 30 minutes on the calendar this week or next? I'd love to show you a quick demo and you can decide from there if it makes sense to continue the conversation. Looking forward to hearing from you!

---

Touch 5 | Email | Day 10

> Subject: Closing the loop...

> Hi {{first_name}}, I've reached out a few times now and haven't heard back. I completely understand - I know you're incredibly busy and have a million things on your plate. I'll respect your time and won't keep bothering you. I'll go ahead and assume this isn't a priority for you right now and close the loop on my end. That said, if anything changes or you'd like to revisit this conversation in the future, please don't hesitate to reach out. I'll be here! Wishing you all the best, and I hope we get the chance to connect someday.

---

**Metrics:** ~8% connection acceptance rate on touch 1, 0% reply rate across touches 2-5. Sent to 60 prospects over 6 weeks.

**ICP:** Head of Sales or VP Sales at 50-300 person B2B SaaS. Company is in a growth phase (Series A-C). Trigger: recently posted a job listing for 3+ SDRs.

---

## Audit

### 1. Critical issues

---

**Touch 1 opens with flattery and zero signal that you know anything about this person or company.**

Cold openers without a specific why-now convert at under 2% acceptance. "Thought leader in the SaaS space" is what every LinkedIn invite note says. There's no hook, no trigger, no reason why this message landed today instead of six months ago. The prospect's brain reads this as automated noise within 3 words.

BEFORE:
```
Hi {{first_name}}, hope this message finds you well! I came across your profile and was really impressed with your work as a thought leader in the SaaS space. We help companies like yours accelerate growth and scale faster.
```

AFTER:
```
Noticed [Company] posted 3 SDR roles in the last 2 weeks. Teams scaling that fast usually run into a ramp problem before the pipeline problem. Happy to share what's worked at similar-stage companies if useful.
```

---

**Touch 2 is a bump sent on day 3 - before any social proof has had time to land.**

Day 3 bumps signal desperation, not persistence. The first message hasn't had enough time to be seen, considered, or deprioritized. A day-3 bump says "I care more about my quota than your attention." It also leads with the lowest-possible-value message: "just wanted to bump this up." There's no new information, no new angle, no reason for the prospect to respond differently than they did to touch 1.

BEFORE:
```
Hi {{first_name}}! Just wanted to bump this up in case it got buried. Would love to chat about how we can help [Company] achieve its goals.
```

AFTER:
```
One thing that usually matters at the SDR hiring stage: ramp time. Companies at [Company]'s growth rate typically see 30-40% of new SDR hires miss quota in month 1 because onboarding tooling wasn't built for volume. Worth a quick look at whether that's a risk here? (One-word reply is fine.)
```

---

**Touch 4 says "I'll keep this brief" then runs 180 words.**

This is a trust violation. The subject sets an expectation ("quick question") that the body immediately breaks. Busy VPs stop reading when the stated contract - "brief" - is violated in the first sentence. Beyond the length, the body contains zero specific information: "companies like Acme Corp and XYZ Inc," "real, measurable results," "I genuinely think this is different" - these are the exact phrases every other vendor uses. This message makes the sender less credible, not more.

BEFORE:
```
I'll keep this brief. I wanted to reach out one more time because I know how busy you are and I wouldn't want you to miss out on something that could really make a difference for your business. Our platform helps companies in the SaaS space streamline their processes, reduce costs, and unlock growth they didn't think was possible.
```

AFTER:
```
Three messages in with no reply. Either the SDR ramp problem isn't live right now, or I got the fit wrong entirely. Both are fair. If ramp time genuinely isn't the issue, one word tells me and I'll stop. If the timing's off, tell me when and I'll reach back then.
```

---

**Touch 5 is a guilt-trip disguised as a breakup.**

"I've reached out a few times now," "I'll respect your time and won't keep bothering you," "I'll go ahead and assume this isn't a priority" - this is not a clean exit. It's a passive-aggressive final ask that makes the prospect feel mildly bad if they don't reply. Experienced buyers have seen this pattern hundreds of times. It doesn't generate replies; it generates mild irritation and an unsubscribe. A clean breakup has one job: close the loop and leave one sentence of door-open. Everything else is noise that undermines the brand.

BEFORE:
```
I've reached out a few times now and haven't heard back. I completely understand - I know you're incredibly busy and have a million things on your plate. I'll respect your time and won't keep bothering you. I'll go ahead and assume this isn't a priority for you right now and close the loop on my end.
```

AFTER:
```
Closing this out - clearly not the right moment. If the SDR scaling challenge resurfaces, you know where to find me.
```

---

**Every touch asks for 30 minutes. The ask never earned it.**

Touches 1 through 4 all close with a request for a 30-minute call. Touch 1 hasn't established relevance yet. Touch 2 hasn't added anything new. Touch 3 is a restatement of touch 1. None of these touches have earned the right to ask for 30 minutes of a VP's time. The ask should escalate: touch 1 earns a reply, touch 2 earns a yes/no, touch 3 earns a short conversation, touch 4 earns a meeting. Asking for a meeting on touch 1 doesn't make the prospect more likely to say yes - it makes them less likely to engage at all.

BEFORE:
```
Could we get 30 minutes on the calendar this week or next? I'd love to show you a quick demo and you can decide from there if it makes sense to continue the conversation.
```

AFTER:
```
Worth a look? (Replying "yes" or "no" takes 5 seconds - either answer is useful.)
```

---

### 2. Smells

- **Tone flatline across all 5 touches:** Every message sounds like a cold opener. Touch 4 and 5 should sound like a different register - warmer, more direct, less "sales." They don't. The prospect receives the same transactional energy on day 10 as day 1, which signals that nothing real happened in between.

- **No new information in touches 2-3:** Touch 2 is a bump with no new content. Touch 3 rewords touch 1. A context-building touch should add one specific piece of evidence - a data point, a company similar to theirs, a question that's genuinely diagnostic. None of these touches do that.

- **Generic social proof:** "Companies like Acme Corp and XYZ Inc" and "hundreds of businesses" with no specifics. Social proof without context is noise. Name the company size, the use case, and the outcome. "A 200-person SaaS in fintech reduced SDR ramp from 90 to 45 days" is proof. "Acme Corp achieved amazing results" is filler.

- **Sequence timing is too compressed:** 5 touches in 10 days for a VP-level target with a non-urgent problem. The SDR hiring trigger is real, but it's not a crisis. Measured timing (day 1, 5, 12, 21, 30) is more appropriate than this pace, which reads as pressure.

- **No branching defined:** What happens if someone replies "not interested"? What happens if they accept the invite but don't reply to touch 2? The sequence has no defined exits. In practice this means prospects who replied and were ignored get touch 3 anyway, which burns the relationship.

---

### 3. What you got right

- **The ICP is concrete.** "Head of Sales or VP Sales at 50-300 person B2B SaaS, Series A-C, actively hiring SDRs" is a usable segment. The hiring-signal trigger is observable and real. This is the right raw material - the sequence just fails to use it. Worth keeping as the foundation for a rebuilt version.

---

**Verdict:** This cadence is repairable in structure but needs a full rewrite of every touch body. The critical issues above address the highest-impact problems. If you apply only the touch 1 and touch 5 fixes first and rerun, you'll have a better signal on whether the ICP and trigger are right before investing in the middle touches. If reply rates remain below 3% after those fixes, the problem is likely ICP fit or trigger quality, not copy - run `icp-definer` before the next rewrite.
